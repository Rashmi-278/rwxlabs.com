# India and institutional Ethereum: a research agenda

###### rwxLabs Research · Ecosystems · India · Draft
###### *Written with AI assistance from the author's prior research. To be reviewed and verified by the author before publication. Regulatory details should be re-checked against current sources at publication time.*

**Audience:** The Ethereum Foundation's enterprise team, Ethereum Institutional, Ethlabs, and institutions with an India strategy.

**Short version:** India has among the deepest pools of Ethereum developers in the world, a government committed to public digital infrastructure, and a regulatory posture toward crypto assets that is cautious but defined. For institutional Ethereum this combination is unusual, and it's under-researched. Here is the agenda we think matters.

---

## Why India, why now

- **Builders:** ETHIndia has been one of the largest Ethereum hackathons for years, and India-based builders show up across ecosystems. In 2026, rwxLabs' founder co-led ETHIndia with Devfolio and programmed its **institutional track**, the ETHIndia Institutional program, alongside Devcon 8 in Mumbai.
- **Public infrastructure DNA:** India built Aadhaar, UPI and ONDC as public rails. Its AI mission is pursuing the same path: decentralized "open cloud compute", micro and edge data centers, and a target of roughly one rupee per inference at population scale. (See our notes from the [Global Tech Summit Innovation Dialogue](https://hackmd.io/@torchablazed/S1A7D0AWZl).)
- **Defined, cautious regulation:** crypto assets ("virtual digital assets") face a heavy tax regime, while the central bank pilots its own digital rupee. Institutions know the rules even though the rules are strict.

## Research questions

**1. Where does public-infrastructure thinking meet Ethereum?**
UPI showed that open, interoperable rails can beat closed platforms. Which institutional use cases, such as trade finance, attestations, credentialing or tokenized deposits, could follow the same "public rail, private apps" logic on Ethereum?

**2. What do Indian institutions actually need?**
We want structured interviews with banks, NBFCs, asset managers and GIFT City IFSC entities about custody, privacy, compliance and settlement requirements. The output would be a needs map, not a pitch.

**3. Can the builder base be redirected?**
India's developers mostly build for global consumer crypto. How many would build institutional infrastructure if there were demand, grants and clear career paths? Grant data from programs funding India-based builders, standardized in DAOIP-5, can show where support goes today.

**4. What is the regulatory path?**
Compare India's approach with other large jurisdictions on custody, tokenization and stablecoins, focusing on what an institution would need to deploy on Ethereum *legally* from India.

**5. Is CROPS a selling point here?**
Does the [CROPS-as-risk-control framing](./06-crops-for-institutions.md) resonate with Indian institutions? Or does it need a local translation, for instance data localization and sovereignty?

## How we'll do it

- **Convene:** Through the Ethereum Bengaluru community hub we run a monthly *Deep Tech × Web3 Explorer* series on how Ethereum lands in regulated Indian industries (law, finance, and more), plus CROPS programming.
- **Measure:** ecosystem scorecards using our [evaluation rubric](./05-how-institutions-should-evaluate-an-ecosystem.md), applied to India-based activity.
- **Publish:** an open report and a dataset, released under open licenses.

## Partners we're looking for

Ecosystem organizations that want an India chapter in their institutional research, and Indian institutions willing to be interviewed under Chatham House rules.
